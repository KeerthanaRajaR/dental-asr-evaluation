Doctor: Hello, good afternoon. I’m Dr. Rahul, the dentist on duty today. What brings you in?

Patient: Good afternoon doctor. I came for a routine dental cleaning, but I also have some discomfort at the back of my mouth.

Doctor: Alright. Let’s talk about the discomfort first. Which side are you feeling it on?

Patient: It’s on the right upper side, near the last tooth.

Doctor: Is the pain constant or does it come and go?

Patient: It comes and goes. Sometimes it feels like pressure, especially while chewing.

Doctor: Any swelling, fever, or difficulty opening your mouth?

Patient: No fever and no swelling. I don’t have any difficulty opening my mouth.

Doctor: That’s good. I’ll do a basic oral examination now. Please open your mouth wide.

Patient: Okay.

Doctor: I see mild plaque accumulation on the lower front teeth and some tartar buildup near the gum line. Your gums look slightly inflamed, but there is no bleeding at the moment.

Patient: Is that serious?

Doctor: No, it’s mild gingivitis. A professional cleaning should resolve it. I don’t see any signs of periodontitis or bone loss.

Patient: That’s a relief.

Doctor: Now, about the discomfort on the right upper side. I see a partially erupted wisdom tooth. Food particles can get trapped there, causing irritation.

Patient: So that’s why it hurts sometimes?

Doctor: Exactly. There’s no active infection right now. No pus, no abscess, and no signs of acute inflammation.

Patient: Will it need to be removed?

Doctor: Not immediately. Since there’s no severe pain or infection, we can monitor it. However, if the pain increases or if swelling develops, extraction may be necessary.

Patient: Okay.

Doctor: Let’s proceed with the dental cleaning. You may feel some vibration and mild sensitivity, but it shouldn’t be painful.

Patient: Alright.

Doctor: I’m starting with ultrasonic scaling now. Let me know if you feel any discomfort.

Patient: It’s a bit sensitive, but manageable.

Doctor: That’s normal. I’m cleaning the lower right quadrant now, followed by the lower left.

Patient: Okay.

Doctor: The entire cleaning will take around 30 to 40 minutes.

Patient: That’s fine.

Doctor: I’m done with the scaling. I’ll now do polishing to remove surface stains.

Patient: My teeth already feel cleaner.

Doctor: Good. After the procedure, avoid eating for at least 30 minutes. Avoid very hot or very cold foods for the next 2 hours.

Patient: Got it.

Doctor: You might experience mild sensitivity today. If needed, you can take paracetamol 500 mg, up to two times a day. Do not exceed 1000 mg in 24 hours.

Patient: I understand.

Doctor: I do not recommend antibiotics at this stage since there is no infection.

Patient: That’s good.

Doctor: For the wisdom tooth area, rinse with warm salt water twice a day. Maintain good oral hygiene and brush gently around that area.

Patient: Should I use any special toothpaste?

Doctor: You can use a desensitizing toothpaste once daily. Avoid aggressive brushing.

Patient: Okay doctor.

Doctor: We’ll review the wisdom tooth in three months. If there is pain on the left side or worsening pain on the right, please come earlier.

Patient: Sure.

Doctor: Do you have any medical conditions like diabetes or hypertension?

Patient: No, I don’t have any medical conditions.

Doctor: Any allergies to medications?

Patient: No known allergies.

Doctor: Perfect. Your dental cleaning is complete. I’ll see you at your next follow-up.

Patient: Thank you so much, doctor.

Doctor: You’re welcome. Take care.
